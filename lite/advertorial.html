What stays within reach

The brush, for the week the sofa loses

The forest-green brush lays a little steam in front of a metal comb. Loose undercoat ends up in the comb. That is the whole claim. If the skin looks angry, stop and call your own veterinarian — this club will not pretend to be one.

The mat, for the bath you keep postponing

The mat sticks to the wall. A xylitol-free smear gives your dog a job while you rinse. Sugar-free jars are the ones to distrust until you have read them. Xylitol is widely published as dangerous for dogs, which is exactly the sort of note the educational helper is for — and exactly the sort of note that ends with "call a veterinarian now," not with a shrug.

The tag, for the stranger who finds them

The collar tag is battery-free QR/NFC. A smartphone camera opens the emergency contact and the med profile you wrote. Scan alerts fire because the page was opened, not because a radio in the tag reported a location. There is no GPS in the tag and no tracking plan to forget to cancel.
